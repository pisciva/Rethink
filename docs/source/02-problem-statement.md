2.1 Problem Background & Evidence

2.1.1 Mathematics Learning Difficulties & Prerequisite Knowledge

Mathematics learning involves interconnected concepts, where understanding more advanced
topics can depend on knowledge established in earlier learning stages. Consequently, difficulties
encountered in a particular mathematics topic may be associated with gaps in the understanding of
relevant prerequisite concepts rather than the target topic alone.

Research discussed in the ReThink ICC 2026 paper supports the importance of prerequisite
knowledge in mathematical learning progression. Preliminary self-reported survey findings also
indicate that difficulties with conceptual understanding and previously learned material are relevant
concerns among the surveyed students.

In an exploratory survey involving 12 high-school students, 10 respondents reported experiencing
mathematics learning difficulties sometimes or frequently, while 6 respondents perceived that some
of their difficulties were associated with previously learned material they had not fully understood.

However, these findings represent students' perceived experiences rather than objective evidence
of prerequisite conceptual blockers. Prerequisite knowledge gaps remain one possible explanation
for mathematical difficulties and should not be assumed to account for every instance of student
struggle.

2.1.2 Difficulty Attribution & Conceptual Understanding

Identifying the source of a mathematical difficulty is not always straightforward. An incorrect answer
may reflect different underlying causes, and answer correctness alone does not necessarily reveal
the conceptual understanding or reasoning involved.

Similarly, students may be able to follow mathematical procedures or worked examples without
consistently demonstrating an ability to apply the underlying concepts independently or across
different problem representations.

Preliminary survey findings indicate that 8 of 12 respondents experienced difficulty understanding
the meaning of mathematical formulas or concepts. Additionally, 7 respondents reported difficulties
when learning examples differed from the problems they encountered, while 5 respondents
reported being able to follow examples but struggling to solve problems independently.

These findings suggest that difficulties in conceptual understanding and independent problem
application are relevant concerns within the surveyed group. However, reported difficulties with
unfamiliar problem forms do not independently establish conceptual misunderstanding or failure of

2.1.3 Evidence Boundaries

The educational literature provides a theoretical foundation for investigating prerequisite knowledge
gaps, difficulties in attributing mathematical errors to underlying concepts, and differences between
procedural performance and conceptual understanding.

Preliminary ReThink user research provides initial supporting evidence that some intended users
experience difficulties understanding mathematical concepts, perceive gaps in previously learned
material, and encounter challenges applying instructional examples to independent problem-solving.

The current evidence consists of an exploratory survey of 12 respondents, predominantly students in
grades XI–XII. The survey uses self-reported experiences and a small, non-representative sample. Its
findings therefore cannot establish the prevalence of these difficulties across high-school students,
objectively confirm conceptual blockers, or demonstrate causal relationships between prerequisite
gaps and learning difficulties.

The extent to which students can accurately identify the source of their mathematical difficulties
also remains insufficiently established.

Further qualitative interviews and task-based mathematical observation are needed to examine the
relationship between perceived  difficulties, observable mathematical reasoning, and potential
prerequisite knowledge gaps. Until stronger evidence is available, conclusions about the target
population and underlying causes should remain provisional.

Students experiencing difficulties with specific mathematics topics may struggle to determine
whether underlying conceptual gaps contribute to their difficulties, identify what understanding
needs to be strengthened, and establish whether they can apply relevant mathematical concepts
independently.

1. Unclear Underlying Learning Difficulties

determine whether the difficulty is associated with gaps in prerequisite conceptual understanding or
other contributing factors.

2.3 Target Users & Segmentation

2.3.1 Primary Target User

ReThink's intended primary users are high-school students experiencing difficulties understanding
or applying mathematics within specific topics, who may benefit from self-directed support in
addressing relevant conceptual gaps.

These students are not required to have previously identified prerequisite knowledge gaps. Their
difficulties may arise from different causes, and the relevance of conceptual recovery depends on
the specific learning difficulty and the conceptual support available.

This target-user definition represents an intended audience rather than a validated user segment. Its
suitability for ReThink requires further investigation through user research and observation of
mathematical learning difficulties.

2.3.2 Initial Research Focus

ReThink's initial user research focuses on students in grades X–XII, with priority given to grades
XI–XII who report difficulties understanding or applying mathematics within their current learning
topics.

In the preliminary exploratory survey involving 12 respondents, 11 participants were students in
grades XI–XII. This composition reflects the  initial research focus and does not establish that
students in these grades experience greater conceptual recovery needs than students in other
grade levels.

The  initial research context emphasizes algebra and related prerequisite concepts to maintain
relevance to ReThink's current MVP focus. However, the selection of this mathematical domain is a

2.3.3 Provisional Segmentation Dimensions

ReThink uses two primary dimensions to explore variation among potential users:

Learning Difficulty

The frequency and characteristics of mathematics learning difficulties students report, including
challenges understanding concepts or applying mathematical knowledge.

Help-Seeking Behavior

The ways students seek, use, or avoid learning assistance when encountering mathematical
difficulties.

Problem Awareness is considered an additional analytical attribute, reflecting how specifically
students can recognize and describe the difficulties they experience.

Preliminary survey findings indicate variation in students' reported learning difficulties and learning
assistance practices. However, these findings do not yet establish distinct, validated, or mutually
exclusive user segments.

Further user research is required to refine the target-user profile and determine which patterns of
learning difficulty, help-seeking behavior, and problem awareness are most relevant to ReThink's
intended users.

2.4 Context of Use & Current Behaviors

2.4.1 Learning Situations & Help-Seeking Triggers

ReThink's intended users may encounter mathematics learning difficulties in different situations,
including challenges following classroom  material, solving specific mathematics problems, or
revisiting previously learned concepts.

Two initial contexts of use are considered relevant:

    ●​  Falling Behind in Class: Students experience difficulties understanding or keeping up with
      mathematics topics currently being taught and may seek additional learning support.
    ●​  Struggling with a Specific Problem: Students encounter mathematics problems they
      cannot solve confidently, even after receiving explanations or reviewing examples.

These contexts remain provisional. Preliminary survey findings indicate that students experience
difficulties across different learning situations, but the available evidence does not establish which
context most strongly motivates them to seek conceptual recovery support.

2.4.2 Current Learning & Help-Seeking Behaviors

Preliminary exploratory survey findings indicate that students use multiple sources of assistance
when encountering mathematics learning difficulties.

Among 12 surveyed high-school students, 10 reported seeking help from teachers, friends, or family
members; 8 used written or video-based learning materials; 7 used AI assistants; and 6 attempted to
solve problems independently. Tutoring services and skipping difficult problems were each reported
by 2 respondents.

These findings indicate that learning support practices within the surveyed group involve a
combination of interpersonal assistance, digital learning resources, and individual problem-solving
efforts.

Reported AI usage suggests  familiarity with AI-based assistance among some respondents.
However,  it does not establish  trust  in AI-generated diagnostic conclusions, preference for
AI-assisted conceptual recovery, or willingness to use ReThink.

The survey also does not establish which learning resources students prioritize, how they combine
different forms of assistance, or which approaches they consider most useful in specific situations.

2.4.3 Self-Directed Learning Conditions

Students' engagement in self-directed mathematics learning may be influenced by their available
study time, motivation, and circumstances surrounding the learning difficulty.

In the preliminary survey, 6 of 12 respondents reported having approximately 15–30 minutes
available for independent study, while 3 reported having no fixed study period. The remaining
respondents reported longer available study durations.

These findings indicate  variation  in reported study-time  availability but do not establish an
appropriate duration for a ReThink learning session.

Self-directed learning in this context refers to students taking initiative in seeking and engaging with
learning support. It does not require students to learn exclusively without teachers, peers, or other
external resources.

Further user research is needed to clarify learning triggers, patterns of help-seeking, and the
conditions influencing students' willingness and ability to participate in a structured conceptual
recovery experience.

2.5 Unmet Needs & Opportunity

2.5.1 Potential Unmet Learning Needs

Preliminary user research  indicates  that some high-school students continue  to encounter
difficulties when using  existing mathematics  learning support,  particularly  in understanding
explanations,  connecting examples  to the problems they  face, and applying mathematical
knowledge independently.

These findings suggest three potential unmet learning needs:

1. Clarity About Relevant Learning Gaps

Students may need greater clarity about which mathematical concepts or areas of understanding
deserve attention when they struggle with a specific topic. However, current evidence is limited
regarding students' ability to identify the underlying causes of their difficulties, and a prerequisite
conceptual gap cannot be assumed from perceived difficulty alone.

2. Conceptually Relevant Learning Assistance

Students may need learning assistance that makes underlying mathematical ideas and reasoning
easier to understand while supporting connections between explanations and the problems they
encounter. Difficulties applying instructional examples may reflect conceptual gaps, differences in
problem complexity, unfamiliar representations, or other contributing factors.

3. Evidence of Independent Application

Students may need  clearer  indications  of whether  they can apply  relevant mathematical
understanding independently rather than relying on their ability to follow instructional examples. This
potential need does not establish that students prefer or are willing to complete additional formal
assessments.

These needs are informed by educational literature and preliminary self-reported survey findings.
Their relative importance, underlying causes, and the extent to which existing learning alternatives
address them remain subject to further investigation.

2.5.2 Core Product Opportunity

The identified learning frictions suggest an opportunity for more focused support that helps
students address potentially relevant conceptual difficulties and better understand the extent of
their independent mathematical application.

This opportunity is particularly relevant where difficulties may involve prerequisite conceptual gaps,
without assuming that such gaps explain every learning challenge.

2.6.2 Key Research Gaps

Four research areas remain relevant to evaluating the assumptions underlying ReThink's problem and
user definition.

1. Observed Mathematical Difficulties

Further task-based mathematical observation is needed to investigate whether students' perceived
learning difficulties correspond to observable prerequisite conceptual gaps, alternative explanations,
or other mathematical learning challenges.

2. Learning Behaviors & Unmet Needs

Additional qualitative research is needed to understand how students recognize learning difficulties,
seek and evaluate available assistance, and determine whether their learning needs have been
addressed.

3. Product Experience Acceptance

Concept testing and usability evaluation are needed to examine whether students understand the
purpose of targeted diagnosis, are willing to engage with the structured learning experience, and
can meaningfully interpret diagnostic outcomes, including inconclusive results and assessment
limitations.

Research should also investigate whether students perceive understanding validation as useful and
acceptable within their learning experience.

4. Learning Outcome Evidence

Appropriate learning evaluation is needed to investigate whether targeted conceptual recovery
helps students independently apply the mathematical concepts being addressed and recognize the
same underlying structures across different problem representations.

2.6.3 Evidence & Decision Boundaries

The current evidence includes educational literature and a preliminary exploratory survey involving
12 high-school students, predominantly in grades XI–XII.

Survey  findings provide  initial evidence of students' reported learning  difficulties, perceived
conceptual understanding challenges, and learning assistance practices. However, the survey's
self-reported nature and limited, non-representative sample do not establish population-level
prevalence,  objectively  confirm  prerequisite  conceptual  blockers,  or  demonstrate  causal
relationships between conceptual gaps and learning difficulties.

The effectiveness of ReThink's diagnostic and recovery approach, students' acceptance of the
proposed learning experience, and the relative importance of the identified unmet needs remain
insufficiently established.

Further findings should be used to evaluate and refine provisional assumptions while maintaining a
distinction between observed user evidence, researcher interpretation, and established product
decisions.

Unresolved methodological and operational decisions are tracked separately in the Product Decision
Register and are not resolved through approval of this section.
